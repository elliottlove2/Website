Reflections, Rotations, and Spinors

Rotating with mirrors

Rotations about the origin and reflections across lines through it form a group called \(\mathrm{O}(2)\). The rotations, made from an even number of reflections, form \(\mathrm{SO}(2)\).

The mirrors are described by their normal vectors: arrows at right angles to the mirrors. Flipping these arrows leaves the mirrors, and their effect on the cat, unchanged, so the result is the same element of \(\mathrm{O}(2)\).

\(\operatorname{Pin}(2)\) keeps track of products of these normal vectors, including their sign. Each element \(g\) in \(\mathrm{O}(2)\) has two representatives in \(\operatorname{Pin}(2)\), differing only by sign. The even products form \(\operatorname{Spin}(2)\).

Loops of Rotations

Two reflections rotate the cat through twice the angle between the mirrors. Here, \(u\) stays fixed while \(w\), the other mirror’s normal arrow, turns at half the cat’s speed.

After the cat travels around 360°, the mirror lines are back where they started, but \(w\) points the other way and \(g=-1\). To get that arrow back to its starting direction, the cat has to travel around twice: 720°. Then \(g=+1\).
