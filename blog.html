Reflections, Rotations, and Spinors

2D
Rotating with mirrors

An object living in a two dimensional plane can be reflected and rotated. In math language, these operations form a group \(\mathrm{O}(2)\). The rotations, made from an even number of reflections, are called \(\mathrm{SO}(2)\).

The mirrors are described by their normal vectors: arrows at right angles to the mirrors. Flipping these arrows leaves the mirrors, and their effect on the cat, unchanged, so the result is the same element of \(\mathrm{O}(2)\).

\(\operatorname{Pin}(2)\) elements are products of these normal vectors, including their sign. Each element in \(\mathrm{O}(2)\) has two representatives in \(\operatorname{Pin}(2)\), called \(g\) and \(-g\), differing only by sign. The even products form \(\operatorname{Spin}(2)\).

Loops of Rotations

Two reflections rotate the cat through twice the angle between the mirrors. Here, \(u\) stays fixed while \(w\), the other mirror’s normal arrow, turns.

After the cat travels around \(2\pi\) radians, the mirror lines are back where they started, the rotation is the same. But the \(w\) vector points the other way around, it is the negative of where it started. The group element in \(\mathrm{SO}(2)\) is the same \(1\), but in \(\operatorname{Spin}(2)\) the group element is \(-1\).

To get the arrows back to normal, the cat has to go around twice, then the Spin element is back to the identity.
